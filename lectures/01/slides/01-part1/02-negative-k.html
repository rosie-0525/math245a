Negative $K$-groups

Bass extended $K$-theory to negative indices:

$$K_{-1}(R),\; K_{-2}(R),\; K_{-3}(R),\; \ldots$$
Definition

\[K_{-1}(R):= \mathrm{coker}(K_0(R[t]) \oplus K_0(R[t^{- 1}]) \to K_0(R[t, t^{-1}])).\]

Recursively define $K_{-n+1}(R)$ from $K_{-n}(R)$.

Key properties
  1. (Bass) If $R$ is Noetherian and regular, then $K_i(R) = 0$ for all $i < 0$. $\rightsquigarrow$ Negative $K$-groups
    measure singularities
  2. (Weibel's conjecture; proved by Kerz–Strunk–Tamme)

    $K_i(R) = 0$ for all $i < -\dim R$.

Dimension 1 — curves


Follows from Weibel's formula: $K_{-1}(C) = \mathbb{Z}^\lambda$, where $\lambda = \#\text{loops in the dual complex of } C$.

Dimension 2 — surfaces

Fact

Let $X=\operatorname{Spec}R$ be a normal surface. Then \[K_{-2}(R) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{\# \text{loops in the dual graph of } E}\] where $E$ is the exceptional divisor of the minimal resolution of $X$.

$A_3$ rational double point

$xy+z^4=0$

$K_{-2}(X)=0$

$D_4$ rational double point

$z^2+xy^2+x^3=0$

$K_{-2}(X)=0$

$T_{2,4,5}$ cusp

$x^2+y^4+z^5+xyz=0$

$K_{-2}(X)=\mathbb{Z}$

Fact: $K_{-2}=0$ for rational double points.

What do we want to know about these groups?

Let $X$ be a variety of dimension $d$.

Question Sample answer
When are they zero?
If $X$ has rational singularities, then $K_{-d}(X) = 0$.
When are they torsion?
If $X$ has rational singularities, then $K_{-d+1}(X)$ is torsion.
Is there an explicit description?
If $X$ has isolated klt type singularities and $d \geq 3$, then $$KH_{-d+2}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} \simeq \operatorname{gr}^W_2 H^d(X, \mathbb{Q}).$$
