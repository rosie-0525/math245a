$\mathbb{A}^1$-invariance $\leftrightsquigarrow$ $K$-theory behaves like a homotopy theory

Theorem (Quillen)

If $X$ is a Notherian regular scheme, then

$$K_m(X)\cong K_m(X\times \mathbb{A}^1) \quad \text{for all } m \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

This fails for singular varieties:

Example (Bloch–Murthy, Weibel)

Let $X=(x^2+y^3+z^7=0)\subset \mathbb{C}^3$. Then $$K_0(X)\not\cong K_0(X\times \mathbb{A}^1).$$

Example (Weibel)

Let $X=(x^2+y^3+z^7=0)\subset \mathbb{C}^3$. Then $$K_{-1}(X) \not\cong K_{-1}(X \times \mathbb{A}^1).$$

However, $$K_{-2}(X) \cong K_{-2}(X \times \mathbb{A}^r),\quad \forall\, r\ge 0.$$

Conjecture (Vorst)

If $X$ is an affine scheme essentially of finite type over a field, then $$\mathbb{A}^1\text{-invariance of } K_n(X) \text{ for all } n \implies X \text{ is regular.}$$

Known for varieties over perfect fields by Cortiñas–Haesemeyer–Weibel and Kerz–Strunk–Tamme.
