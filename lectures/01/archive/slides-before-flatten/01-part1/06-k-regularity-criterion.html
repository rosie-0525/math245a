When is a variety $K_m$-regular?

Theorem

Let $X = (f(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = 0)\subset \mathbb{C}^n$ be a hypersurface with isolated singularities. Then

$$X \text{ is } K_m\text{-regular} \iff \tilde{\alpha}_f \ge \left\lceil{\frac{m+n-1}{2}}\right\rceil,$$

where $\tilde{\alpha}_f$ is the minimal exponent of $f$.

Example

Let $f = x^2+y^3+z^7$. Then $\tilde{\alpha}_f = \tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{3}+\tfrac{1}{7} = \tfrac{41}{42} < 1$.

By the theorem, $R$ is $K_m$-regular iff $\tilde{\alpha}_f \geq \lceil \tfrac{m+2}{2} \rceil$:

$m$ $\lceil \tfrac{m+2}{2} \rceil$ $K_m$-regular?
$-2$ $0$ ✓
$-1$ $1$ ✗
$0$ $1$ ✗
$1$ $2$ ✗
$2$ $2$ ✗
Corollary

For a complex hypersurface $X$ of dimension $d$ with isolated singularities, we have:

  • $X$ is $K_{-d}$-regular (Holds in general by Cortiñas–Haesemeyer–Schlichting–Weibel and Kerz–Strunk–Tamme)
  • $K_{-d+1}\text{-regular} \iff K_{-d+2}\text{-regular}$
  • $K_{-d+3}\text{-regular} \iff K_{-d+4}\text{-regular}$
    $\cdots$
Remark

This fails for:

  • Non-isolated singularities:
    $X = (xyzwu=0) \subset \mathbb{C}^5$ is $K_1$-regular but not $K_2$-regular.
  • Non-hypersurfaces:
    Cone over $C \times \mathbb{P}^1$ (for $C$ a genus $g$ curve, in sufficiently ample embeddings) is $K_{-2}$-regular but not $K_{-1}$-regular.
