Why the two definitions agree

Theorem
For $(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{E})$ with $\mathcal{C}$ additive: Weibel's definition $\iff$ Quillen's axioms (a)–(c).

($\Rightarrow$) Check (a)–(c) inside $\mathcal{A}$ (Weibel, Ex. II.7.8):

  • kernels, cokernels, pullbacks and pushouts exist in $\mathcal{A}$, and closure under extensions brings them back into $\mathcal{C}$.

($\Leftarrow$) Gabriel–Quillen embedding ($\mathcal{C}$ small):

  • $\mathcal{A} = $ additive $F\colon \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathrm{Ab}$ that are left exact: $0 \to F(C) \to F(B) \to F(A)$ is exact for each sequence in $\mathcal{E}$. The embedding is Yoneda, $B \mapsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, B)$.
  • $\mathcal{A}$ = abelian sheaves on $\mathcal{C}$, where the coverings are the admissible epis (a topology because of (b)), so $\mathcal{A}$ is abelian.
  • $\operatorname{Hom}(-, B)$ is a sheaf because $j = \operatorname{coker} i$; $\operatorname{Hom}(-, j)$ is an epimorphism of sheaves because $j$ is a covering.

Quillen omits the details. Full proof: Bühler, Exact categories, App. A (after Thomason–Trobaugh).
