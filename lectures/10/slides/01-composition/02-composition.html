Composition in $Q\mathcal{C}$

Given $X \twoheadleftarrow Y' \rightarrowtail Y$ and $Y \twoheadleftarrow Z' \rightarrowtail Z$, take the fiber product $P = Y' \times_Y Z'$:

$P$ $Z'$ $i'$ $Z$ $\lrcorner$ $j'$ $X$ $j$ $Y'$ $i$ $Y$

The composite $X \to Z$ is

$$X \twoheadleftarrow P \rightarrowtail Z \qquad (P \to Y' \to X \text{ and } P \to Z' \to Z).$$
Question
Why is the fiber product $P$ well-defined?

Answer: it is built into the definition of an exact category.
