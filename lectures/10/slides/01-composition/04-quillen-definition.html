Exact categories: Quillen's definition

Definition (Quillen 1973, §2)
An exact category is an additive category $\mathcal{C}$ with a class $\mathcal{E}$ of sequences $0 \to A \xrightarrow{i} B \xrightarrow{j} C \to 0$ (short exact sequences; $i$ is an admissible mono $\rightarrowtail$, $j$ an admissible epi $\twoheadrightarrow$) such that:
  1. $\mathcal{E}$ is closed under isomorphism and contains $0 \to A \to A \oplus C \to C \to 0$. For each sequence in $\mathcal{E}$, $i = \ker j$ and $j = \operatorname{coker} i$ in $\mathcal{C}$.
  2. Admissible epis are closed under composition and under base change by arbitrary maps. Dually, admissible monos are closed under composition and under cobase change by arbitrary maps.
  3. If $f\colon B \to C$ has a kernel in $\mathcal{C}$ and $B' \to B \xrightarrow{f} C$ is an admissible epi for some $B' \to B$, then $f$ is an admissible epi. Dually for admissible monos.
  • “Base change exists” is part of (b): for $B \twoheadrightarrow C$ and any $C' \to C$, the pullback $B \times_C C'$ exists in $\mathcal{C}$, and $B \times_C C' \twoheadrightarrow C'$ is again an admissible epi.
  • (c) follows from (a) and (b) (Yoneda, Keller; see Bühler, Prop. 2.16).
