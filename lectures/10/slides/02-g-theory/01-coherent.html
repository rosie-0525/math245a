$G$-theory uses coherent modules

Definition
Let $\operatorname{Coh}(R)$ be the category of coherent $R$-modules, i.e. finitely generated modules whose finitely generated submodules are all finitely presented. $$G_i(R) := K_i(\operatorname{Coh}(R)).$$ Likewise $G_i(X) := K_i(\operatorname{Coh}(X))$ for a scheme $X$.
  • When $R$ is noetherian, all finitely generated $R$-modules are coherent.
  • In general, we use $\operatorname{Coh}(R)$ because it is abelian.
Example
$R$ and $R/I$ are finitely generated $R$-modules, but the kernel $I$ of $R \to R/I$ need not be: e.g. $R = k[x_1, x_2, \ldots]$, $I = (x_1, x_2, \ldots)$.
